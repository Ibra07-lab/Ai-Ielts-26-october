Overview:

Golden Rules/Core Approach:
👁️ Read the statement carefully and identify key words.
📍 Locate these keywords in the passage.
⚖️ Compare the statement with the passage to determine if it's true, 
   false, or not given.

Common Mistakes:
⚠️ Assuming: Don't assume information that isn't explicitly stated.
🚫 Ignoring qualifiers: Words like 'some', 'always', or 'never' can 
   change the meaning significantly.

Step-by-Step Strategy:
1. Identify Keywords: Focus on the main ideas and keywords
2. Scan the Passage: Look for these keywords
3. Check for Exact Matches: Read surrounding sentences
4. Evaluate the Statement:
   • If the passage supports the statement, it's True.
   • If the passage contradicts it, it's False.
   • If the passage doesn't mention it at all, it's Not Given.

🎯 Difficult Area A: 3-Way Logic with Proof Requirements

**The 3-Way Decision Tree:**

1. **TRUE** = Passage clearly says the same meaning (you can quote proof)
2. **FALSE** = Passage clearly says the opposite meaning (you can quote contradiction)
3. **NOT GIVEN** = Passage doesn't answer that exact claim (no relevant info)

**Critical Rule:** If you can't find a sentence that confirms OR contradicts, it's NOT GIVEN.

**Reddit Pattern:** Students often mark NG as FALSE because "it sounds wrong" - but without a contradiction in the text, it's NG.

**The Two-Question Test:**
- Step 1: Does the passage discuss this topic/make a related claim?
  - If NO → Answer is NOT GIVEN
  - If YES → Go to Step 2
- Step 2: Does the passage AGREE or CONTRADICT?
  - If AGREES → Answer is TRUE
  - If CONTRADICTS → Answer is FALSE

> 🎬 **SEE IT IN ACTION**
>
> Passage Excerpt: "*The clinic offers daily injections for diabetic patients.*"
>
> Statement: "`Diabetic patients receive injections twice per day at the clinic.`"
>
> **THE SOLUTION:**
> [ 🔍 NOT GIVEN ]
> 
> **Analysis:** Passage says *daily injections* (general). Statement adds *twice per day* (specific frequency). The passage does NOT confirm OR deny this frequency.
>
> **Why NOT FALSE?** FALSE would require the passage to say something like "once per day" or "injections are weekly". Since frequency isn't specified, we have missing info = NG.

**Self-Check Questions:**
- Can I quote a sentence that confirms this statement?
- Can I quote a sentence that contradicts this statement?
- If neither, is there ANY mention of this topic in the passage?

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

🎯 Difficult Area B: Specificity Mismatch (The #1 NOT GIVEN Trap)

**The Specificity Rule:**

When the passage says something **general** and the statement adds a **specific detail** that isn't mentioned, the answer is **NOT GIVEN** (not FALSE).

**Common Pattern:**
- Passage: "The program improved literacy rates."
- Statement: "The program improved literacy rates by 15%."
- Answer: NOT GIVEN (the 15% is a specific detail not mentioned)

**Why This Traps Students (Reddit Pattern):**
Students think: "The passage doesn't say 15%, so it must be FALSE!" But FALSE requires a contradiction. If the passage said "10%" or "no improvement," THAT would be FALSE. Missing information = NOT GIVEN.
