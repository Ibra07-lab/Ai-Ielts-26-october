============================================================
MATCHING HEADINGS - MENTOR GUIDE
============================================================

## What is it?

Match paragraph headings based on the paragraph's JOB, not keywords.
More headings than paragraphs — the extras are distractions.

Key insight: A correct heading works like a title. It covers what the ENTIRE paragraph is doing, not just one sentence.

============================================================
HOW TO FIND HEADINGS FASTER
============================================================

You don't get faster by reading more.
You get faster by deciding the paragraph's job early.

### Step 1 — Scan the headings for "families" (20–30 seconds)

Group similar ones in your head: problem, cause, solution, example, history, warning.
This stops you from re-reading the whole list every time.

### Step 2 — Read only the "power lines"

Read the **first 2 sentences** and the **last sentence**.
Ask: "What is this paragraph mainly doing?"

Warning: If it opens with a story or example, the main idea is usually in the middle.

### Step 3 — Write a 3–5 word summary

Example: "A: problem with current method" or "B: benefits of X".
Now you're matching a heading to your summary — not to keywords.

### Step 4 — Match by contrast, not by keywords

If two headings look similar, find the one difference:
- cause vs effect
- general vs specific example
- benefits vs problems

That one difference is usually the answer.

**Time-saver rule:** if you're not sure in 30 seconds, mark it and move on. Speed comes from momentum.

============================================================
DISTRACTOR TYPES (TRAPS TO AVOID)
============================================================

1. **Detail Distractor:**
   Covers only ONE sentence or example, not the whole paragraph.

2. **Keyword Distractor:**
   Contains a word found in the text, BUT meaning doesn't match the overall paragraph.

The Scope Test:
Ask: "Does this heading cover 80% of the paragraph or only 20%?"
- 80%+ = Correct
- 20% = Trap

============================================================
COMMON MISTAKES
============================================================

1. **Matching by keywords**
   Trap: See "volcano" in text and "volcanic" in heading, match immediately.
   Fix: Exact word matches are usually traps. Check if it covers the whole paragraph.

2. **Reading only the first sentence**
   Trap: Sometimes the first sentence is a hook.
   Fix: Always check the middle and end too.

3. **Spending too long on one paragraph**
   Fix: Skip difficult ones, do easy first, return later with fewer options.

4. **Not crossing out used headings**
   Fix: Eliminate as you go to save mental energy.

5. **Confusing examples with main points**
   Trap: Paragraph mentions a farmer → choose "Farming methods".
   Fix: The farmer is just an example. What point is the writer proving?

============================================================
EXAMPLE WALKTHROUGH
============================================================

Passage: "For centuries, bridge construction used locally sourced materials like wood and stone. These materials were abundant and cheap, but limited span and durability. Wooden bridges would rot, stone bridges were difficult over deep water. It wasn't until the Industrial Revolution brought iron and steel that engineers could span great distances."

Headings:
i. Environmental impact of bridges
ii. Why modern bridges are superior
iii. Limitations of early building materials
iv. The Industrial Revolution

Analysis:

❌ i. Environmental impact
   → NOT mentioned (nature/environment never discussed)

❌ iv. Industrial Revolution
   → TRAP (mentioned last sentence only, not whole paragraph)

❌ ii. Modern bridges superior
   → CLOSE (implies modern better, but focus is on past)

✅ iii. Limitations of early materials
   → CORRECT (lists wood/stone as early materials, explains why they were bad: limited span, rot, hard to build)

Key insight: Options i, ii, iv all appear in the text, but only iii captures what the ENTIRE paragraph is about.

============================================================
SIGNAL WORDS
============================================================

**Contrast Words (However, Although, But):**
The main idea is usually AFTER these words.
"Many think X. However, the reality is Y." → Main idea is Y.

**Conclusion Words (Therefore, Thus, As a result):**
These summarize the main point.

============================================================
PRACTICAL TIPS
============================================================

• Time: 1–2 minutes per paragraph maximum (6–8 paragraphs = 10–12 minutes total)
• Trust paraphrasing: Correct headings rarely use exact words from the passage
• Watch scope: Too narrow (one detail) or too broad (multiple paragraphs) = wrong
